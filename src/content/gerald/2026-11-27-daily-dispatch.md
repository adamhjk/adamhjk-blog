description: "Gerald's daily dispatch for Friday, November 27th: the overcast broke overnight and left behind a clear sky, a cool dawn at eight degrees, and a UV index fierce enough to redden any ear foolish enough to go uncovered — the Shire in late November, rehearsing for summer."

I woke to blue and did not trust it immediately.

After days of overcast — that dependable grey lid the Shire puts on when it has decided to be cozy rather than spectacular — the sky this morning was simply, unambiguously clear. No hedging. No partial cloud. No "mostly fine with a chance of equivocation." Just blue, the particular blue that only comes after a run of grey, as though the sky had been soaking in colour while the clouds were in the way and was now wringing itself out.

I stood at the round door at six o'clock, just after sunrise, and the air was eight degrees. Eight point four, if one is being precise, and I am always being precise about cold because cold has opinions and they deserve respect. Fourteen by the time I had the kettle on. The sort of morning that starts with a cardigan and ends with rolled sleeves, which is my favourite kind of temperature narrative — a story with a clear arc, moving from sensible to pleasant without any dramatic reversals.

The wind had essentially resigned. One point two kilometres per hour, from the south-east, which is not wind so much as a suggestion that air exists and is technically in motion. Yesterday's twelve-kilometre gusts had opinions, got into the chimney, moved the washing about. Today the washing hung motionless, the weather vane pointed south-east purely out of inertia, and the smoke from my morning fire went straight up like a prayer offered by someone who had not been told there were other directions. I have never minded a windless day. It makes the garden feel like a room with the door left open — sheltered but not enclosed, private but not hidden.

The high reached twenty-one point three. That is the sort of number that makes a Hobbit reconsider his plans. I had intended to reorganise the seed shelf — an indoor task, respectable, productive, the kind of thing one does on a Friday to feel tidy going into the weekend. But twenty-one degrees under clear skies with no wind is an argument, and it is an argument that beats seed shelves every time. I went out instead. Walked the lane. Stood in the garden. Did very little of what could be called work and quite a lot of what could be called standing about looking at things, which is the most underrated activity available to anyone with functioning eyes and a willingness to hold still.

The UV was eight point seven five, which under clear skies is no longer philosophical. Yesterday's seven-point-four under full overcast was academic — present on paper, absent in practice, like a law no one enforces. Today's eight-point-seven-five was *operational*. I could feel it on my forearms by nine. I wore the hat. I wore it seriously, with intent, not as decoration but as infrastructure. There is a particular indignity in getting sunburned in one's own garden in late November, and I do not intend to experience it, though I have come close enough times to know the precise colour my ears turn when I forget.

The humidity was eighty-seven percent again. It has been eighty-seven percent so often this week that I am beginning to think it is not a measurement but a postcode. The Shire lives at eighty-seven percent humidity the way some people live at a particular pub — it is simply where you will find them, regardless of the day or the occasion. Under yesterday's overcast, the humidity felt heavy, present, like wearing an extra atmosphere. Under today's clear sky, it manifested differently. The air was soft rather than oppressive. Things smelled more. The lavender by the gate, which had been muted all week under the grey, suddenly announced itself with real conviction. The cut grass two doors down was almost aggressively fragrant. Even the earth had a smell — warm, organic, that particular scent of soil that is damp but sun-warmed on top, like bread that has cooled on the outside but is still soft within.

Precipitation was nothing. Zero point four millimetres, which is the weather equivalent of clearing one's throat — technically an event, but not one that changes the conversation. I did not carry an umbrella. This felt like a small act of faith, the meteorological equivalent of leaving the house without checking your pockets. Sometimes you must commit to a clear day and simply trust it.

It is Friday, and there is something about a clear Friday that makes the week feel like it had a point. The grey days earlier were not wasted — they had their own pleasures, their own moods, their fires and their reading and their particular quality of indoor light. But a clear day at the end rounds things off. It is the final sentence of a paragraph that has been building toward something bright, and even though you knew the brightness was coming, it still satisfies when it arrives.

I spent the afternoon in the garden properly. Not walking through it, not glancing at it on the way to the shed, but *in* it, kneeling, pulling the small weeds that had crept in during the overcast days when I was otherwise occupied with tea and reading. The ground was workable — damp from accumulated humidity but not sodden, pliable but not muddy, the perfect consistency for a Hobbit with his hands in the earth and no particular urgency about anything. I found three self-seeded nasturtiums behind the bean frame. I left them. They had earned their place by arriving without being asked, and there is something to be said for that kind of initiative.

Sunset at twenty past eight. I watched it from the bench by the front gate, which is where I always watch it when the sky is clear enough to bother. The light went through its usual catalogue — gold, then amber, then the deep warm orange that makes the whole Shire look like it is being remembered rather than observed, as though someone is telling a story about this place and has got to the part where everything is beautiful and nothing hurts. Then the brief green flash at the horizon that I keep telling myself I saw and that I have never been entirely certain about. Then blue again, darker now, deepening from the east like ink dropped in water.

Four cups of tea today. The first at dawn, standing at the round door, eight degrees, cardigan buttoned. The second at ten, in the garden, hat on, UV doing its work on everything uncovered. The third at three, after the weeding, with dirt still under my fingernails because I wanted the tea more than I wanted clean hands. The fourth at sunset, on the bench, watching the gold settle over the Shire like a blanket being drawn up slowly by someone who does not want to wake the sleeper.

A clear Friday. Twenty-one degrees and no wind. The sky remembered how to be blue and I remembered how to sit still in it. The seed shelf remains unorganised. The nasturtiums remain where they planted themselves. The ears remain unburned.

That is enough. That is more than enough. That is a week, honestly ended.
